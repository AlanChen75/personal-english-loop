Our primary outcome is load-state detection, not energy attribution. For each of the 9 devices, we compare its predicted on and off states with test labels and count true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. From those 4 counts we calculate the Matthews correlation coefficient, or MCC. We then take the simple arithmetic mean of the 9 device MCC values. Each device receives equal weight, regardless of its duration or power rating. Event F-one is secondary. This distinction matters: the main number is not a single pooled confusion matrix, and it is not the aggregate-only score used to pick the configuration. The test labels are used only after selection is locked.
