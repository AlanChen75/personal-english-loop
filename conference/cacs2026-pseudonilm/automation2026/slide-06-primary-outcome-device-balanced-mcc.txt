Our primary outcome is load-state detection, not energy attribution. For each of the nine devices, we compare its predicted on and off states with test labels and count true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. From those four counts we calculate the Matthews correlation coefficient, or em see see. We then take the simple arithmetic mean of the nine device em see see values. Each device receives equal weight, regardless of its duration or power rating. Event F-one is secondary. This distinction matters: the main number is not a single pooled confusion matrix, and it is not the aggregate-only score used to pick the configuration. The test labels are used only after selection is locked.
